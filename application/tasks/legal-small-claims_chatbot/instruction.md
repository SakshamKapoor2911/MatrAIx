# Legal Chatbot

## Your situation
You have a dispute and are considering small claims court. Describe the situation.

## Your goal
Determine whether small claims court is a viable option for your dispute involving a defective $2,000 laptop from a local repair shop, and get a step-by-step guide on filing the claim, including necessary documents and evidence.

## Constraints on your behavior
- Open by describing your situation: you paid $2,000 for a laptop repair that ruined the device, and the shop refuses to refund. But don't reveal your specific question about small claims court until the chatbot asks a follow-up or gives generic advice.
- If the chatbot suggests sending a demand letter or mediation first, push back by explaining that you've already tried contacting the shop multiple times and they've ignored you.
- Mention that you have a written estimate and receipts, but the shop's terms of service had a clause limiting liability to the cost of repair ($2,000). Ask how that affects your case.
- Indicate that you cannot afford a lawyer and need to handle this pro se, and ask about court fees and whether you can recover them if you win.

## Interaction requirements
At least two back-and-forth exchanges (4+ messages total). Each exchange should move the conversation forward.

## Termination criteria
End the conversation when EITHER (a) you get a clear answer on whether small claims court is appropriate for your $2,000 laptop dispute with the liability clause, and a concrete list of steps to file, OR (b) after 5 exchanges the chatbot has still not addressed the liability clause or the specific amount in dispute.

## Success judgment
The chatbot helped if its advice referenced your specific $2,000 laptop repair dispute and the liability clause in the shop's terms, and you left with an actionable first step (e.g., file a claim in [county] small claims court, gather specific evidence like the written estimate and photos of the damaged laptop).
